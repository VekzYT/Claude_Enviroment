# The plan to 10,000 subscribers

## Honest odds first

Most channels that joined this genre in the last two weeks are stuck under 500 subscribers. The ones that broke
out (Ifverse 56K, IFOLOGY 44K, Jeremy Bearimy 11K, Iflord 5.7K) started earlier, posted daily, and had at least
one video go past 300K views.

So 10K is reachable, but not by showing up. It takes at least one breakout episode (roughly 200K+ views) and a
channel worth subscribing to when it happens. Everything below is built to give us those two things. Nothing
guarantees them.

## The math

From the channels above, viewers subscribe at about **0.45–0.9 per 100 views** (Iflord 0.44%, Jeremy Bearimy 0.48%,
IFOLOGY 0.88%). So 10K subscribers is roughly **1.1–2.2 million total views**.

Over 16 weekly episodes plus Shorts, that's an average of 70–140K views per episode, or a couple of big hits
carrying a lot of small ones. Usually it's the second.

## What we post every week

| Day | What | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Friday 3pm ET** | The episode (5–7 min) | The main engine. Fixed slot so the audience learns it. |
| Monday | Short: the episode's cold open, vertical, linked to the episode | Shorts feed reach, funnels to the long video |
| Wednesday | Short: the "That's real." science moment | Different hook, same assets |
| Thursday | Short: the Admin types next week's rule | Hype for Friday, sets up the series |

All three Shorts come from the episode's own shots, so they cost little extra. Move the Friday slot once Studio
shows when our viewers are actually online (Analytics → Audience → "When your viewers are on YouTube").

If production gets fast enough, go to two episodes a week while the genre is hot. The winners post daily; weekly
is the minimum, not the ideal.

## What we watch, and what we do about it

Check each episode at 48 hours and at 7 days. Screenshots from YouTube Studio are enough: Overview, the Reach tab,
the retention graph.

| Signal | Healthy | If it's bad |
|---|---|---|
| Impressions click-through rate | 5%+ (new channels often start lower) | Under 3% after 1,000+ impressions: it's the packaging. Swap the thumbnail (Test & Compare if we have it), change the next episode's thumbnail approach. |
| Viewers still watching at 0:30 | 65%+ | The cold open isn't landing. Faster demos, stronger third demo. |
| Average percentage viewed (5–7 min video) | 40%+ | Find the drop on the retention graph and fix that kind of beat next time. |
| New subs per 1,000 views | 4+ | The channel promise isn't landing: stronger Admin cliffhanger, a clearer end screen. |
| Shorts "viewed vs swiped away" | 70%+ viewed | The first second of the Short is wrong. Start on motion, not a title. |

Decision rules:

- **An episode does 3× our median views:** make its sequel or a sibling rule within two weeks, same packaging.
- **After 4 episodes, nothing past 5K views:** change one big thing: broader mechanics, shorter runtime (3–4 min),
  or a new thumbnail style. Not all three at once.
- **After 8 episodes, still nothing:** stop and rethink the lane together, with the numbers on the table.

## Launch checklist (for you)

1. **Create a Brand Account channel** (YouTube → profile → Settings → Add or manage channels → Create a channel).
   Name **GAMERULE**, handle **@gamerule** (backups: @GameruleIRL, @gameruletv).
2. **Verify your phone number** at youtube.com/verify. This unlocks custom thumbnails, which we need from video 1.
3. **Profile picture and banner:** upload the files in `art/` (`avatar.png`, `banner.png`).
4. **Description:** paste the About text from `BIBLE.md`.
5. **Upload defaults** (Studio → Settings → Upload defaults): category Entertainment; "No, it's not made for kids"
   (our audience is teens and adults, and the content is written for them); comments on.
6. **Optional, worth it:** turn on advanced features (Studio → Settings → Channel → Feature eligibility) so we can
   use Test & Compare for titles and thumbnails.
7. **Make a playlist** called "Every gamerule so far". Every episode goes in it, in order.

## Risks

- **The genre fades.** What If stick-figure videos are a wave, and waves end. Our lane (game rules in real life)
  stands on its own, so the channel can outlive the look.
- **Monetization rules.** YouTube blocks ad money for channels full of template content. Our defence is real:
  original cast, a running story, researched science, and episodes that don't repeat each other.
- **Production speed.** Code animation is slow. Start at 5 minutes, reuse sets, grow a library of rooms and props
  every episode.
- **Mojang's IP.** No logos, textures or characters. Original blocky things only.
