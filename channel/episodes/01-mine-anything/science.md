# Episode 1: the science

Every number in the script, worked out, with where it comes from. Checked 28 Oct 2026.

## Real facts

**1. A one-meter block of stone weighs 2.7 tonnes.**
Granite's density is about 2.65–2.75 g/cm³. 1 m³ × 2,700 kg/m³ = 2,700 kg = 2.7 t.
Source: standard material density tables (e.g. Engineering ToolBox, "Densities of solids").

**2. A stack of 64 weighs 173 tonnes, as heavy as a blue whale.**
64 × 2.7 t = 172.8 t ≈ 173 t.
Blue whales: the heaviest recorded was 190 t (a female, 1947); adults are commonly 100–160 t. 173 t is in the
range of a large blue whale, so "as heavy as a blue whale" holds.
Sources: Guinness World Records, "Largest animal"; Britannica, "Blue whale".

**3. A full inventory is 6,200 tonnes, most of the Eiffel Tower.**
36 × 64 = 2,304 blocks. 2,304 × 2.7 t = 6,220.8 t.
Eiffel Tower: metal frame 7,300 t, total 10,100 t. 6,221 t is 85% of the frame and 62% of the whole tower, so
"most of the Eiffel Tower" holds either way.
Source: toureiffel.paris, "Key figures".

**4. One block of water is one tonne.**
Water's density is about 1,000 kg/m³ (998 at 20 °C). 1 m³ ≈ 1 t.

**5. In real life, one block of water is a puddle.**
A backyard pool of 4 m × 8 m × 1.5 m holds 48 m³. One cubic meter spread over its 32 m² floor is 1/32 m ≈ 3 cm
deep. (Dex's "about fifty more" is the 47 still missing.)

**6. Ronan Point, 1968.**
16 May 1968, Canning Town, East London. A gas leak exploded in a corner flat on the 18th floor of the 22-storey
tower, blowing out its load-bearing outer walls. The floors above lost support and fell, and the debris brought
down the floors below: one entire corner collapsed. 4 people died, 17 were injured. It's the textbook case of
"disproportionate collapse" and changed building codes worldwide.
Sources: Wikipedia, "Ronan Point"; UK National Archives, "Ronan Point collapse".

**7. One kilometer down: about 25 °C hotter.**
Near the surface, the geothermal gradient in most of the world is about 25–30 °C per km.
Source: Wikipedia, "Geothermal gradient"; British Geological Survey.

**8. Four kilometers: as deep as the deepest mine on Earth; the rock is 60 °C; ice keeps the miners alive.**
Mponeng gold mine, South Africa, operates at around 4 km (3.9 km by 2012, deeper since). Rock at the face reaches
60–66 °C. Surface plants make about 5,000–6,000 t of ice a day, sent underground to keep tunnel air below 30 °C.
Sources: Wikipedia, "Mponeng Gold Mine" (66 °C); IIR, "Mine cooling" (5,000 t of ice a day, 60 °C at the face);
Guinness World Records, "Deepest mine" (6,000+ t of ice slurry).

**9. The deepest hole ever: 12 km, 180 °C at the bottom, three times deeper than your 4 km.**
Kola Superdeep Borehole, Russia, reached 12,262 m in 1989. The bottom was about 180 °C (they had expected 100 °C).
12,262 / 4,000 ≈ 3.1.
Source: Wikipedia, "Kola Superdeep Borehole".

**10. Two-tenths of one percent of the way to the center.**
Earth's mean radius is 6,371 km. 12.262 / 6,371 = 0.19%.

**11. Head first, you fall faster.**
Belly-down terminal velocity is about 200 km/h; head-down freefly is about 240–290 km/h. Skydivers change body
position to speed up or slow down and meet each other in the air.
Source: FAI Skydiving Commission; Wikipedia, "Speed skydiving".

**12. Cliff divers: 27 m, about 85 km/h.**
Red Bull Cliff Diving men's platform is 27 m (women 21 m). Divers hit the water at up to about 85 km/h in under
3 seconds. (Free fall from 27 m: √(2 × 9.81 × 27) = 23 m/s = 83 km/h, which matches.)
Sources: Wikipedia, "Red Bull Cliff Diving World Series"; Red Bull fact sheet.

**13. "You're doing over two hundred."**
Two people falling together, one head-down and one belly-down, sit between about 200 and 290 km/h (fact 11).
"Over two hundred" is the conservative end.

## Things the story makes up (and that's fine)

- The gamerule, the five hits, the inventory holding 6,000 t without weighing anything, the Admin.
- Surviving the dig. A real 4 km shaft would be unbearably hot and could collapse; we skip past it in a line.
