# Episode 1: What If You Could Mine Anything?

- **Command:** `/gamerule mineAnything true`
- **Logline:** You can punch anything into a block and carry it, until you find out what a real block weighs, why
  buildings don't float, and why every player knows never to dig straight down. And someone is watching.
- **Length:** about 570 words of narration, about 4:30 with the silent beats. The breakout channels' first videos ran
  3:30–6:30; a tight pilot beats a padded one.
- **The power:** word **"Mine."** · sound **crack, crack, pop**, then a soft pickup blip · look: cracks spread over
  the thing in stages, it collapses into a small block that bobs and spins, then zips to you and lands in the hotbar.
- **Cast:** You, Dex, Juni, Mom, the Admin (chat only), a car owner, a teacher.
- **Rule of the episode:** only mining is game logic. Everything else, weight, gravity, heat, falling, is real.
- **Every number** is worked out in `science.md`. Don't change one without changing it there.

Temperatures, distances and speeds appear on screen in both units (°C/°F, km/mi, km/h/mph). The narration says
metric.

**S2 · macro insert.** Your fist hits it. Cracks spread.

**S3 · insert.** Crack, crack, it collapses into a tiny bobbing block. *Pop.* Silence.
> Mine.

**S4 · wide · street.** The school bus pulling away without you.
> Missed the bus? Don't go around the school.

**S5 · medium · school wall.** You punch the brick. A door-shaped hole, two blocks high; the bricks pop into cubes

**S6 · wide · classroom.** You step out of the new hole and sit down. The teacher freezes mid-sentence. *(no line)*

**S7 · wide · parking lot, dusk.** A car parked right across your bike.
> Someone parked on your bike.

**S8 · close-up.** Your knuckles on the car door. Crack. Crack.
> Mine.

**S9 · medium.** The whole car is a block the size of a dice, bobbing over your palm. Behind you, the owner, keys in

**S12 · insert.** The yellow line again, close.

**S16 · close-up.** The slow grin.
> You test it on your bedroom wall.

**S17 · wide · outside the house.** A perfect square hole in your bedroom wall; you lean out of it into the morning

> It works.

## Beat 4: the cost (1:45–3:20)

### Problem one: weight

**S27 · schematic.** Dark grid. A cube outline, labelled 1 m.
> Here's the first problem. In the game, a block is one meter on each side.

**S28 · schematic.** The cube fills with granite. Counter: 2.7 t.
> A real one-meter block of stone weighs 2.7 tonnes.

**S29 · schematic.** 64 cubes stack up; the counter rolls to 173 t; a blue whale silhouette slides in beside them,
the same size.
> A stack of sixty-four? 173 tonnes. As heavy as a blue whale. That's real.

**S30 · schematic.** The inventory grid fills, 36 slots; the counter rolls to 6,221 t; the Eiffel Tower's outline
appears.
> Fill all thirty-six slots and you're carrying six thousand two hundred tonnes. Most of the Eiffel Tower.

**S31 · medium · your bedroom.** You stand there, perfectly fine. You bounce on your heels. Not a creak.
> And you feel nothing. Your floor doesn't even creak.

**S32 · close-up.** The cube backpack, glowing faintly.
> Which means something, somewhere, is holding all of it for you.

### Problem two: gravity

**S33 · wide · parking garage.** You mine one block out of a concrete pillar to cut through.
> Problem two. In the game, blocks float. Here, they don't.

**S34 · low angle.** Cracks race across the ceiling. Dust falls. A deep groan. *(no line, let the sound do it)*

**S35 · schematic.** A 22-storey tower. One flat on the 18th floor lights up and its outer walls blow out; the
corner above and below it folds down, floor by floor.
> In 1968, a gas explosion blew out the walls of one flat in a twenty-two-story tower in London. A whole corner of
> the building came down, floor by floor. That's real.

**S36 · close-up.** You slap the block back into the pillar. The cracks stop.
> You put the block back. Fast.

### Problem three: never dig straight down

**S37 · medium · backyard, night.** Dex shining his phone torch into a one-by-one hole.
> Problem three is Dex again. "How deep can you go?"

**S38 · close-up.** You, looking down the hole.
> Every player knows the rule. Never dig straight down.

**S39 · top-down.** You drop into the hole, mining as you go. Top left, coordinates: `Y: -100`.
> You dig straight down.

**S40 · medium · the shaft.** `Y: -1000`. The rock walls glow faintly warm. Sweat.
> One kilometer. It's twenty-five degrees Celsius hotter than the surface. Every kilometer adds about that much
> again.

**S41 · close-up · the shaft.** `Y: -4000`. Red-brown rock, steam off your hoodie. On screen: 60°C / 140°F.
> Four kilometers. You're as deep as the deepest mine on Earth. The rock is sixty degrees. Too hot to touch.

**S42 · schematic.** A cutaway of a real deep mine: tunnels, and ice pouring down a shaft.
> The miners who work down here only survive because thousands of tonnes of ice get pumped down every day. That's
> real.

**S43 · schematic.** Your 4 km shaft beside a line three times as long, labelled 12,262 m · 180°C.
> The deepest hole humans have ever drilled goes three times deeper. Twelve kilometers. At the bottom, it was a
> hundred and eighty degrees.

**S44 · schematic.** Pull back: the whole Earth. Both holes are a scratch on the surface.
> And even that is two-tenths of one percent of the way to the center of the Earth.

**S45 · medium · the shaft.** You placing blocks under your feet, climbing. Exhausted.
> You climb back up. It takes all night.

> In the game, a water block saves you. In real life, cliff divers jump from twenty-seven meters and hit the water
> at about eighty-five kilometers an hour. You're doing over two hundred.
