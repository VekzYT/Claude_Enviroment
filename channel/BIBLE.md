# GAMERULE: channel bible

The rules every episode follows. Change them on purpose, never by drift.

## The promise

**Real life just got game rules.** Every episode, someone types a new `/gamerule` into reality and you have to live
with it. We follow it all the way: the fun, the real science of what would actually happen, and the moment it goes
wrong.

Why this, not generic superpowers: see `research/2026-10-28-market-scan.md`. Short version: game-mechanic episodes
beat everything else on the channel that tried them, Minecraft's mechanics are untouched by the genre, and the
generic-superpower lane is flooded with clones.

## Name and handle

- **GAMERULE**, handle **@gamerule** (free on 28 Oct 2026). Backups: @GameruleIRL, @gameruletv.
- Never put "Minecraft" in the channel name, logo or handle (Mojang's brand). Titles and descriptions can mention it
  where it's true.

## Who it's for

Teens and young adults who play games, Minecraft above all, and already binge the What If genre. They know what
`keepInventory` means, they've died to fall damage, and they'll call out a wrong mechanic in the comments within a
minute. Write for them; the science keeps everyone else watching.

## The hook: the command

Every episode opens with the same signature beat, right after the cold open:

> A chat line types itself in the corner of the screen: `/gamerule mineAnything true`
> Then, in yellow: `Gamerule mineAnything is now set to: true`

That's our title card. Nobody else in the genre has it. Gamers recognise it instantly; everyone else reads it as
"a rule just changed".

Rule names follow Minecraft's real style (camelCase: `keepInventory`, `fallDamage`, `doDaylightCycle`). Real ones
where they exist, invented ones where they don't (`mineAnything`).

## The lore: the Admin

Someone has admin access to reality. Nobody knows who.

- The Admin only ever appears as **red chat text**: `[Admin] interesting.`
- Beat 5 of the formula ("someone powerful notices") is always the Admin noticing you.
- **Every episode ends with the Admin typing the next episode's command.** The next rule is the cliffhanger, the
  end-screen tease and the reason to subscribe.
- Never reveal who the Admin is. Let the comments theorise. Drop small clues; keep a list in `lore.md` once we
  have a few episodes.

## The look

The genre's proven grammar, plus our signature layer.

- **Proven:** dusk palette (purple to orange), light from things you can see (windows, lamps, screens), dark
  mid-tones, rim light on the character, depth (blurred dark foregrounds), fast cuts that change shot size, almost
  no on-screen text.
- **Ours, the HUD:** game UI drawn over real life, sparingly.
  - Chat box, bottom left: commands in white, system lines in yellow, the Admin in red.
  - Hotbar, bottom centre: appears when the rule involves items. Counts in the corner of each slot.
  - Coordinates, top left: `Y: -4000` during a dig, that kind of thing.
  - Achievement toast, top right: once an episode, for the funniest beat.
- **Colours:** lime `#8BE04E`, deep green `#2F7D32`, chat yellow `#FFD93B`, Admin red `#FF4D4D`, dusk
  `#2B1E3F` to `#F08A4B`.
- **Type:** HUD in a pixel face (Pixelify Sans); titles and thumbnails in a heavy sans.

## Writing

- Second person, present tense, short sentences (3–8 words). Every line is a picture.
- The power has a **word, a sound and a look**. Ep 1: "Mine." + crack-crack-pop + cracks spreading, then a tiny
  bobbing block.
- Demos escalate: small and selfish, then useful, then a little uncanny.
- **Every number is real.** Work it out and write the source in the episode's `science.md` before it goes in the
  script. Say "That's real." after the big one.
- Game details must be right. If a mechanic is off, the comments will say so, and they'll be right. When unsure,
  check with the human on the team (they play).
- End on the Admin and the next command. Never on a resolution.

## Episode shape (our seven beats)

| Beat | What | Long version |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cold open: three demos | 0:00–0:25 |
| 2 | The command types itself (title card), then the rules | to ~1:00 |
| 3 | The fun: escalating wins, Dex's ideas | to ~2:00 |
| 4 | The cost: real science, real numbers, "That's real." | to ~3:30 |
| 5 | The Admin notices | ~4:30 |
| 6 | Juni is in danger; you choose | ~5:10 |
| 7 | The Admin types the next rule | last 20 s |

Target 5–7 minutes to start (that's what the breakout channels' first videos ran). Go longer only when retention
says the audience wants it.

## Titles

- Genre grammar so we get suggested next to the winners: `What If You Could Mine Anything?`
- Power first, game word second. The game word does the differentiation: *mine*, *respawn*, *inventory*, *fall
  damage*.
- Never reuse a title a What If channel already has. Search YouTube first (see Topics).
- Test two titles per episode with Test & Compare once it's available on the channel.

## Thumbnails

- One character (You), one idea, one or two huge words; the last word in yellow.
- The player marker visible above the head: it's the brand.
- One game element that tells the story (a cracking block, a depth counter, a hotbar).
- Dark dusk background, lime hoodie as the brightest colour after the text.
- Readable at phone size. If the idea needs the title to make sense, redo it.

## Topics

1. A game mechanic everyone recognises.
2. Real science behind the cost (physics, biology, economics, engineering).
3. **Search YouTube first.** If a What If channel already has that title, change the rule or drop it.
4. **Don't take ideas from a plain "give me what-if ideas" prompt.** The clones all do that and land on the same
   list. Ideas come from playing the game, from the comments, and from the backlog.
5. No real people, no brands as the subject, no famous characters.

## Never

- Copy another channel's character, title, script or thumbnail.
- Use Mojang's logo, textures or characters (no Steve, no creepers). Original blocky things only.
- Make distressing-for-views content (animals or kids actually hurt, gore). Danger resolves.
- State a fact we haven't checked.
- Claim any other channel is made with any particular tool.
- Post something template-identical to the last episode. Each one needs its own sets, demos and turn. That's both
  good craft and YouTube's monetization rule.
